# From Restarting Japanese to Reading a Physical Tankōbon

This is the primary learning plan. Its objective is not to finish an app, pass a
test, complete a flashcard deck, or translate manga with assistance. Its
objective is:

> Open a physical Japanese tankōbon, read a complete chapter without digital
> assistance, follow the story and most dialogue directly, and postpone the few
> necessary lookups until after the scene or chapter.

The first target is **Dragon Ball volume 1 in Japanese**. Later validation must
use an unfamiliar chapter or volume so that memory of the story cannot imitate
Japanese comprehension.

## Starting assumption

Start operationally from near zero:

- Do not assume old kanji, vocabulary, or grammar is available on demand.
- Do expect relearning to be faster because earlier study left dormant traces.
- Never skip a foundation because it looks familiar; skip it only after using it
  correctly in unseen Japanese.
- Do not restart WaniKani, Bunpro, or another unbounded review queue.

This distinction matters. Dormant knowledge is an advantage, not a prerequisite.

## Definition of success

The first major goal is achieved when all of the following hold across three
chapters:

- The principal events, speakers, intentions, and jokes are understood.
- At least 75–80% of bubbles have an adequate first-pass gist.
- Ten or more pages can be read before consulting anything digital.
- Necessary lookups average roughly three or fewer per page.
- Unknown minor language can be tolerated without losing the story.
- A short chapter summary remains accurate after a delay.

Perfect translation is not required. Reading means constructing the story in
Japanese despite some uncertainty.

## Why the path has several tracks

Physical manga reading combines:

1. Immediate kana recognition.
2. Recognition of vocabulary as complete words.
3. Connection of kanji words to their readings.
4. Grammar parsing without word-by-word translation.
5. Casual contractions, omitted subjects, and sentence endings.
6. Manga layout, speaker tracking, visual inference, and sound symbolism.
7. Enough automaticity to hold a whole scene in working memory.
8. The ability to continue when comprehension is incomplete.

Research on L2 Japanese manga reading identifies situational interpretation,
speech variation, sentence endings, onomatopoeia, manga-specific grammar, and
dictionary strategy as separate needs. Kanji knowledge alone is therefore not a
route to manga literacy.

The program uses four continuing tracks:

- **Structure:** a systematic elementary grammar course.
- **Words:** vocabulary and kanji readings learned together.
- **Fluency:** easy, continuous reading below the frustration threshold.
- **Transfer:** regular attempts at the physical target manga.

No track is allowed to replace the others.

# Operating rules

## Time, not debt

Use a time budget. Do not use an ever-growing obligation list.

### Minimum day — 15 minutes

- 5 minutes: retrieve a few existing words or patterns.
- 5 minutes: one tiny grammar exercise.
- 5 minutes: one easy Japanese page.
- Add no new material.

### Standard day — 40–50 minutes

- 10 minutes: bounded retrieval.
- 15 minutes: structured course work.
- 15 minutes: graded or manga reading.
- 5–10 minutes: listening or a physical manga attempt.

### Long day — 60–90 minutes, optional

Add reading, not more reviews. A long session is never required to compensate
for a missed day.

## Anti-burnout rules

- One day each week has no deliberate Japanese study.
- Review ends after 10–15 minutes even when items remain.
- After missed days, resume; never catch up.
- Add at most 5–8 deliberate new words on a study day.
- Mine at most three items from a manga page.
- Suspend items that repeatedly fail and are not currently useful.
- Do not study isolated kanji for this reading goal.
- Do not translate complete chapters line by line.
- Do not maintain simultaneous grammar courses.
- If three consecutive pages are below 50% comprehension, reduce difficulty.
- Manga appears every week from the beginning; it is not a distant reward.

## The evidence rule

A skill is considered active only when it works in unseen material.

Examples:

- Seeing a grammar explanation and thinking “I remember this” is not mastery.
- Correctly interpreting it in two new sentences is evidence.
- Recognizing a kanji meaning is not enough.
- Recognizing a useful word, its reading, and its role in a sentence is evidence.

# Stage 0 — Relearn the writing system

Typical effort: **15–30 hours**. Advance by evidence, not schedule.

## Objectives

- Read hiragana without romanization.
- Read katakana without romanization.
- Recognize common combinations, small kana, long vowels, and doubled consonants.
- Read short words as units rather than decoding one character at a time.

## Sequence

### Hiragana

Learn and retrieve:

- Basic rows.
- Dakuten and handakuten: が, ざ, だ, ば, ぱ rows.
- Small や／ゆ／よ combinations: きゃ, しゅ, ちょ, and so on.
- Small っ.
- Long vowel patterns.
- Common particles whose pronunciation differs: は, へ, を.

### Katakana

Repeat the same sequence, then add:

- Long vowel mark ー.
- Foreign-sound combinations such as ティ, ファ, ウィ.
- Familiar loanwords encountered in manga.

## Daily method

1. Learn one small group.
2. Retrieve it from memory, both directions.
3. Read real words containing it.
4. Read one extremely easy sentence.
5. Stop after 15–20 minutes.

Handwriting may be used briefly to distinguish characters, but writing recall is
not a graduation requirement.

## Exit gate

- A mixed kana chart can be read with at least 95% accuracy.
- A short kana-only sentence can be read without romanization.
- Recognition is mostly immediate; persistent pauses are limited to rare
  katakana combinations.

If the gate fails, continue short word-reading sessions rather than repeatedly
memorizing the chart.

# Stage 1 — Elementary Japanese I

Primary spine: **Genki I, third edition, Lessons 1–12**, or the free Japan
Foundation **Irodori Starter and relevant Elementary 1 material** if purchasing
Genki is undesirable.

Do not complete both. Genki is the default because it provides a single ordered
sequence with dialogues, grammar, exercises, reading, writing, and audio. Its
publisher places Volume I around CEFR A1/JLPT N5. The full Genki sequence is
designed as roughly 200 classroom hours, but self-study and dormant knowledge
will change the actual time.

Typical effort for this stage: **80–130 hours**.

## Required structural syllabus

Learn these through the chosen course in its own order:

- X は Y です and question formation.
- これ／それ／あれ and この／その／あの.
- Core particles: は, が, を, に, で, と, へ, も, の.
- Time, location, existence, and movement.
- Verb groups and present/past affirmative/negative forms.
- い- and な-adjectives.
- Frequency, quantity, counters, and comparison.
- て-form formation.
- Requests with てください.
- Permission and prohibition.
- Connecting actions and descriptions.
- ている for ongoing action and state.
- Plain forms.
- Informal speech.
- Quotation with と.
- と思う and と言う.
- ないでください and obligation patterns.
- Dictionary-form nominal modification.
- Preferences, ability, experience, and intention.
- Explanatory の／ん.

## Course lesson protocol

Each lesson is a multi-day unit:

1. Listen to the dialogue without text.
2. Read the dialogue with text.
3. Learn only the lesson's high-value vocabulary.
4. Study one grammar group.
5. Complete enough exercises to retrieve it.
6. Read the lesson's reading passage.
7. Write or say three original sentences.
8. Revisit the dialogue several days later.

Do not finish every workbook question merely for completion. Continue until the
forms work, then move on.

## Vocabulary and kanji protocol

Learn lexical items in this form:

> 魚（さかな） — fish  
> ひさしぶりに魚にしよっと。  
> “I’ll go with fish for the first time in a while.”

Every useful item contains:

- Printed form.
- Reading.
- One core meaning.
- One short example.
- Optional audio.

Kanji are learned through these words. A character's broader meanings and other
readings can be added later when new words require them.

### Retrieval policy

- New items: 5 per normal day, occasionally up to 8.
- New-item days: at most five days per week.
- Review timer: 10 minutes normally, 15 maximum.
- If reviews feel heavy for three days, add no new items.
- After an absence, review a small current subset and archive or postpone the
  rest. A queue is a recommendation, not a moral debt.

## Reading begins immediately

Start Tadoku Level Start/0 material during the first week. Move upward whenever
the current level is comfortable. Read for story, not analysis.

For easy reading:

- Do not translate.
- Do not make cards.
- Avoid dictionaries unless one word blocks the whole story.
- Change books when reading stops flowing.

## Physical manga contact

Once per week, spend 10 minutes with physical Dragon Ball:

1. Look at one page.
2. Identify panels and bubble order.
3. Read anything recognizable.
4. Infer the scene from the artwork.
5. Stop before it becomes an hour-long decoding exercise.

At this stage the manga attempt is measurement and motivation, not the primary
source of comprehensible input.

## Stage 1 exit gate

- Genki I competence or equivalent is demonstrated in unseen examples.
- Tadoku Level 1–2 stories can usually be followed without translation.
- Ordinary short sentences can be segmented into grammatical parts.
- Common verb and adjective forms are recognized.
- Some Dragon Ball bubbles are understood directly, even if the page is not.

# Stage 2 — Elementary Japanese II and reading volume

Primary spine: **Genki II, Lessons 13–23**, or Irodori Elementary 1–2 and
Pre-Intermediate material. Genki's publisher places Volume II around CEFR A2 or
JLPT N4.

Typical effort: **110–180 hours**.

## Structural syllabus

The exact lesson order follows the chosen course. Ensure the following become
usable:

- Potential forms.
- そう for appearance and hearsay where introduced.
- てみる, ておく, てあげる／くれる／もらう.
- Desire, plans, and intention.
- Relative clauses of increasing length.
- し, から, ので, のに.
- なら, と, ば, and たら conditionals.
- Volitional forms.
- Transitive and intransitive pairs.
- てしまう.
- Honorific and humble language at recognition level.
- Passive.
- Causative.
- Causative-passive at recognition level.
- ように and ようになる.
- はず, かもしれない, and other probability expressions.

## Add the spoken-manga layer

Textbook Japanese alone is insufficient. Begin a small notebook or reference
list for forms such as:

- ている → てる
- てしまう → ちゃう／じゃう
- ておく → とく
- では → じゃ
- なければ → なきゃ
- なくては → なくちゃ
- という → って
- のだ → んだ
- もの → もん
- というのは／っていうのは
- Sentence endings よ, ね, な, ぞ, ぜ, さ, わ, かな, かしら
- Colloquial omissions and contractions encountered in the target manga

Add a form only after seeing it in a full sentence. Learn its function, not an
isolated English label.

## Two reading lanes

### Lane A — easy extensive reading

Three sessions per week, 15–25 minutes:

- Tadoku at a comfortable level.
- No mining.
- Almost no lookup.
- Move down a level when reading becomes laborious.

### Lane B — supported close reading

Two sessions per week, 20–30 minutes:

- Short learner text or one approachable manga page.
- Attempt the whole passage first.
- Investigate only blocking language.
- Select at most one grammatical structure and three words.
- Reread after understanding.

## Listening support

Listening is not the main goal, but it strengthens word readings and casual
language segmentation.

Two or three times per week:

- Listen to 5–10 minutes of level-appropriate Japanese.
- First listen for gist.
- Then use Japanese text or subtitles.
- Listen once more without English.
- Do not mine everything.

When using anime, choose a short familiar scene. Anime is support, not evidence
that printed Japanese can be read.

## Stage 2 exit gate

- Core elementary grammar is parsed reliably.
- Tadoku Level 2–3 material generally flows.
- At least half of an approachable Dragon Ball page has an adequate unaided gist.
- Most failures can be classified as particular words, readings, contractions,
  or patterns rather than “the whole sentence is opaque.”
- A page can be revisited and understood without consulting its translation.

# Stage 3 — The native manga bridge

Typical effort: **100–180 hours**.

The physical tankōbon now becomes a major learning source, but easy reading
continues. Removing easy reading at this point often produces slow decoding
without fluency.

## Three weekly reading modes

### 1. Extensive reading

Two or three sessions per week. Read easy Japanese continuously for 20 minutes.

### 2. Intensive physical manga

Three sessions per week. Begin with one or two pages.

Protocol:

1. Read the complete physical page without tools.
2. Mark each bubble:
   - `✓` understood
   - `~` partial/uncertain
   - `?` unable to parse
3. Give a one-sentence page summary.
4. Only then verify uncertain language.
5. Classify each failure:
   - vocabulary
   - forgotten reading
   - grammar
   - contraction/casual speech
   - speaker/layout
   - visual or cultural inference
6. Keep no more than three transferable items.
7. Hide all help and reread the physical page.
8. Reread it again after two or three days.

### 3. Pleasure preview

Once per week, look ahead through several physical pages without studying them.
Follow the artwork and read whatever is accessible. This protects the identity
of manga as entertainment.

## What deserves deliberate retention

Keep an item only if one or more applies:

- It has appeared multiple times.
- It is common general Japanese.
- It explains several future sentences.
- It caused an important misunderstanding.
- Its reading is likely to recur.
- It is characteristic of a recurring character and useful for following them.

Usually discard:

- One-off proper names.
- Most sound effects.
- Rare joke-specific wording.
- Isolated kanji meanings.
- Complete sentence translations.
- AI-generated cultural trivia.

## Intermediate grammar acquired on demand

As it appears, learn patterns such as:

- わけ／わけではない
- はず／はずがない
- ことになる／ことにする
- ようだ／みたい
- まま
- ばかり
- ところ
- のに／くせに
- なんて／なんか
- しか／さえ
- に違いない
- ということ
- ものだから
- わりに
- ほど／くらい

Use a grammar reference for clarification. Do not begin another comprehensive
course unless repeated gaps show that the elementary spine was insufficient.

## Lookup budgets

The budget counts deliberate interruptions, not quick checks performed after
the page.

- First month: no strict cap, but finish the page before checking.
- Next target: no more than 8 essential lookups per page.
- Then 5.
- Then 3.

The objective is not to suppress useful learning. It is to develop inference and
continuity.

## Stage 3 exit gate

Across three chapters:

- Main events and intentions are understood on the first pass.
- 75–80% of bubbles receive `✓` or a strong `~`.
- Ten pages can be read before checking.
- Essential lookups average about three or fewer per page.
- Delayed summaries remain accurate.
- Reading is slow but feels like following a story rather than solving a cipher.

# Stage 4 — Read the first physical tankōbon

Read Dragon Ball volume 1 from beginning to end.

## Rules

- Physical Japanese is the only surface used during first-pass reading.
- Mark difficult page numbers on a slip of paper.
- Check only after a scene, then after a chapter.
- Do not create study items during the first pass.
- At chapter end, give a short spoken or written summary.
- Continue through minor uncertainty.
- Investigate only gaps that changed the story or are likely to recur.

## Second pass

After finishing the volume, reread it physically:

- Make no preparations.
- Compare reading continuity and lookup frequency with the first pass.
- Notice whether expressions are recognized directly rather than remembered from
  an English answer.

## Transfer test

Read an unfamiliar, approachable shōnen chapter in Japanese. It must not be an
episode or chapter remembered closely from Italian or anime.

Passing the transfer test means:

- The central narrative is followed.
- Unknown language does not stop the chapter.
- Assistance is postponed until afterward.
- The summary is substantially correct.

This is stronger evidence than rereading a familiar Dragon Ball volume.

# Stage 5 — Expand across the collection

Approximate personal difficulty ladder:

1. Dragon Ball
2. Naruto, Bleach, Yu Yu Hakusho
3. One Piece, Soul Eater, Fairy Tail
4. Death Note, Bakuman, Genshiken
5. Monster, Vinland Saga
6. Berserk, Vagabond, Sidooh

This is not absolute. Familiarity, dialogue density, invented terminology,
dialect, historical language, and availability of furigana all change the
difficulty.

Do not begin with Vagabond or Sidooh merely because they are favorites.
Historical and stylistic language would conflate foundational reading problems
with genre-specific difficulty. They remain destination works.

# First twelve weeks

The stage gates override this calendar. Repeat weeks rather than advancing with
unstable skills.

## Weeks 1–2 — Kana and restart

- Hiragana, then katakana, in small groups.
- Read real words from day one.
- Begin Tadoku Start material.
- Look at one Dragon Ball page each week without trying to master it.
- Establish the 15-minute minimum-day habit.

## Weeks 3–4 — First sentence structure

- Begin Genki Lesson 1 and proceed slowly.
- Learn approximately 20–30 useful words per week.
- Continue Tadoku Start/0.
- Listen to course dialogue audio.
- Perform one weekly manga observation.

## Weeks 5–6 — Verbs and particles

- Continue the course at demonstrated pace.
- Practise sentence segmentation.
- Read easy material three times per week.
- Read individual accessible manga bubbles, not complete translations.

## Weeks 7–8 — Conjugation and descriptions

- Retrieve present, past, negative, adjective, and particle patterns.
- Start recognizing several kanji words through vocabulary.
- Reread an earlier graded story and compare fluency.
- Attempt one Dragon Ball page cold; record only the failure categories.

## Weeks 9–10 — て-form foundation

- Give て-form sufficient time; it supports many later constructions.
- Continue course audio and easy reading.
- Learn no more than three items from the weekly manga page.

## Weeks 11–12 — Consolidation

- Add no grammar for several days.
- Revisit unseen exercises from prior lessons.
- Read several graded stories.
- Repeat the cold Dragon Ball page diagnostic with different pages.
- Compare bubble comprehension and failure categories with Week 1.

At Week 12, continue from actual course position. Do not force completion of
Genki I on a calendar deadline.

# Progress record

Track only information that changes decisions.

## Weekly record

```text
Week:
Focused minutes:
Easy reading minutes:
Physical manga pages attempted:
Bubble gist: __ / __
Essential lookups per page:
Main failure category:
One thing that became easier:
Next week's adjustment:
```

## Monthly physical reading check

Use five previously unread consecutive Dragon Ball pages.

Record:

- Time to finish the cold pass.
- Number of ✓, ~, and ? bubbles.
- Accuracy of page summaries.
- Essential lookups.
- Breakdown of failure causes.

Do not reuse the same pages for consecutive monthly checks.

## Meaningful metrics

- Consecutive physical pages before checking.
- Percentage of bubbles with adequate gist.
- Essential lookups per page.
- Proportion of failures caused by vocabulary versus grammar.
- Accuracy after a delayed reread.
- Minutes of easy continuous reading.

## Metrics to ignore

- Streak length.
- Number of isolated kanji “learned.”
- Total cards created.
- Percentage completion of a tool.
- Hours spent translating.
- WaniKani or JLPT label without transfer to actual pages.

# Role of KomaYomi and other tools

KomaYomi is optional verification infrastructure. It is not the curriculum and
must not become the primary reading surface.

Appropriate uses:

- Verify OCR after a cold physical attempt.
- Select an unknown word after finishing the page.
- Investigate one blocking grammatical expression.
- Compare an interpretation with the source.
- Keep a small number of transferable lessons.
- Recheck difficult pages after reading a scene or chapter.

Inappropriate uses:

- Hover every bubble before attempting it.
- Translate the page sequentially.
- Ask AI for every sentence.
- Treat successful assisted comprehension as reading ability.
- Accumulate explanations without retrieving anything later.

The app is removed from first-pass reading during Stages 3 and 4.

# Recovery procedures

## After missing several days

1. Do one minimum day.
2. Add no new words.
3. Read something easier than the previous material.
4. Resume the current grammar lesson; do not restart the course.
5. Archive excess reviews if necessary.

## After missing several weeks

1. Spend one week on retrieval and easy reading.
2. Test the last three grammar areas with unseen examples.
3. Resume at the first area that fails.
4. Keep manga contact tiny but present.
5. Do not calculate or repay a backlog.

## When manga becomes miserable

- Reduce intensive pages.
- Increase easy reading.
- Choose a dialogue-light Dragon Ball scene.
- Stop mining low-frequency words.
- Check whether the actual gap is grammar rather than vocabulary.
- End the session while curiosity remains.

## When flashcards take over

- Stop new cards for a week.
- Cap review at 10 minutes.
- Delete cards with no clear future reading value.
- Replace card time with easy reading.
- Remember that cards support encounters; encounters are the goal.

# Resource stack

Use the smallest sufficient stack.

## Primary

- **Genki I and II, third edition:** one structured elementary spine.
- **Free Tadoku books:** easy reading from Start through Level 5.
- **A physical Japanese Dragon Ball volume:** permanent target and transfer
  practice.
- **A Japanese dictionary with inflection support:** selective lookup.

## Reference, not another course

- **A Dictionary of Basic Japanese Grammar:** clarification when a course or
  manga construction remains unclear.
- **Japanese the Manga Way:** optional bridge for manga examples and registers.

## Free primary-course alternative

- **Irodori Starter, Elementary 1–2, and Pre-Intermediate:** Japan Foundation
  A1–B1 materials with audio, worksheets, and authentic or quasi-authentic daily
  reading. Use instead of Genki, not alongside it as a second obligation.

# Immediate next action

Begin Stage 0. The first session is deliberately small:

1. Spend 10 minutes testing hiragana recognition.
2. Relearn only the first failing group.
3. Read five real words containing that group.
4. Read one Tadoku Start page.
5. Open Dragon Ball for five minutes, observe one page, and stop.

The path advances from one recognized word, to one parsed bubble, to one page,
to one scene, to one chapter, and finally to one physical tankōbon. Nothing is
postponed until an app or course declares Japanese “complete.”
